NREMT Progressive Case Study: OB/GYN

Topic: Third Trimester Vaginal Bleeding

Cognitive Level: 3–4 (Application, Analysis, Clinical Judgment)

Phase 1: Initial Dispatch

You are dispatched to a private residence for a 31-year-old female with vaginal bleeding. On arrival, the patient is sitting on the couch holding her abdomen. She states:

"I'm 35 weeks pregnant. About 20 minutes ago I started bleeding."

She appears anxious.

Initial Vital Signs:
BP: 108/72 mmHg HR: 104/min RR: 20/min SpO₂: 98% (RA)
Skin: Warm, pale, slightly diaphoretic
Question 1: What should be your highest priority at this point?

Phase 2: New Information

During your assessment, the patient tells you:

  • Bleeding is bright red
  • She has no abdominal pain
  • She has had no trauma
  • She has soaked one sanitary pad
  • She reports normal fetal movement
Question 2: Which condition is most likely?

Phase 3: Patient Deterioration

While preparing for transport, the patient suddenly becomes pale and dizzy. Bleeding continues.

Updated Vital Signs:
BP: 90/58 mmHg HR: 124/min RR: 24/min SpO₂: 97%
Question 3: Which intervention is most appropriate?

Phase 4: Complication

During transport, the patient states:

"I think I'm having contractions."

Contractions are every 2 minutes. You observe crowning.

Question 4: What should you do next?

Phase 5: Delivery

The infant delivers. The newborn:

  • Is limp
  • Is not crying
  • Heart rate is 80/min
  • Has poor muscle tone
Question 5: What is the highest-priority intervention?

Phase 6: Clinical Judgment Reflection

1. What finding most strongly suggested placenta previa rather than abruptio placentae?
2. Which finding indicated the patient was developing hypoperfusion?
3. Why is rapid transport essential in this case?
