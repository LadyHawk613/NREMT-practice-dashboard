NREMT Progressive Case Study
Environmental Emergencies: Heat Stroke

Dispatch Information

You are dispatched to a local hiking trail for a "person feeling weak and dizzy after hiking."

Weather conditions:

  • Temperature: 102°F (39°C)
  • High humidity
  • Patient has been hiking for several hours

You arrive and find a 45-year-old male sitting beside the trail. He appears exhausted and anxious.

Question 1: Scene Size-Up

Which action should the EMT perform first?

Correct Answer: C

The EMT must ensure the scene is safe before patient contact. Environmental emergencies may place rescuers at risk.

NREMT Thinking:
"Before I treat the patient, is the environment safe for me?"

New Information

The scene is safe.

The patient states:

"I was hiking for about four hours. I started feeling dizzy, but I thought I could push through it."
  • Skin: Cool and moist
  • Respirations: 24/min
  • Pulse: 118/min
  • Blood Pressure: 102/68 mmHg
  • SpO₂: 98%
  • Alert and oriented ×4

Question 2: Patient Classification

Which condition is most likely?

Correct Answer: B

The patient has heat exhaustion:

  • Heavy sweating
  • Weakness
  • Dizziness
  • Normal mental status

Heat stroke is suspected when altered mental status develops.

Condition Changes

The patient suddenly becomes confused.

He says:

"I need to get back to the trail... my dog is waiting."
  • Skin: Hot
  • Pulse: 132/min
  • Respirations: 30/min
  • Blood Pressure: 94/58 mmHg
  • Temperature: 105°F (40.5°C)

Question 3: Treatment Priority

What is the EMT's priority action?

Correct Answer: B

The patient now has heat stroke:

  • Altered mental status
  • Hot skin
  • Temperature above 104°F

Rapid cooling is the priority.

Further Information

The patient becomes increasingly confused and begins vomiting.

Question 4: Oral Fluids Decision

What should the EMT do regarding oral fluids?

Correct Answer: C

Altered mental status creates an aspiration risk. Do not give oral fluids.

Final Assessment

  • GCS decreases from 14 to 10
  • Respirations become shallow
  • Pulse remains rapid

Question 5: Airway/Breathing Decision

What is the most appropriate next action?

Correct Answer: A

Continue:

  • Rapid cooling
  • Airway monitoring
  • Oxygen support as needed
  • Rapid transport

NREMT Key Takeaways

Heat Exhaustion:

  • Sweating
  • Weakness
  • Dizziness
  • Normal mental status

Heat Stroke:

  • Altered mental status
  • Hot skin
  • Temperature greater than 104°F
  • Rapid cooling required

NREMT Trigger:
Hot environment + confusion = Heat Stroke → COOL NOW
