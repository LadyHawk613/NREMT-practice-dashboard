NREMT EMT Review: Patient Assessment & Clinical Decision Making

Assessment
The systematic process of gathering information about a patient.

NREMT: Follow the assessment sequence to identify life threats.
Scene Size-Up
The first step of patient assessment involving scene safety, PPE, MOI/NOI, and resources needed.
Mechanism of Injury (MOI)
The force or event that caused a traumatic injury.

Example: MVC, fall, explosion.
Nature of Illness (NOI)
The medical problem causing the patient's emergency.

Example: chest pain, stroke symptoms.
Primary Assessment
Rapid assessment used to identify and treat immediate life threats.

Airway → Breathing → Circulation
General Impression
The EMT's first overall observation of the patient based on appearance, position, and distress level.
AVPU
Level of consciousness assessment:
A = Alert
V = Verbal
P = Pain
U = Unresponsive
Airway
The passage that allows air to move into and out of the lungs.

Always assess first in the ABC sequence.
Breathing
Assessment of respiratory rate, quality, and effort.

Look for respiratory distress or failure.
Circulation
Evaluation of pulse, bleeding, and perfusion.

Includes identifying shock.
Perfusion
The delivery of oxygenated blood to body tissues.

Poor perfusion may indicate shock.
Chief Complaint
The patient's main reason for seeking medical care.
SAMPLE
Patient history mnemonic:

Signs/Symptoms
Allergies
Medications
Past history
Last oral intake
Events
OPQRST
Symptom analysis tool:

Onset
Provocation
Quality
Region/Radiation
Severity
Time
Secondary Assessment
Detailed assessment performed after immediate threats are addressed.
Reassessment
Repeating assessments to identify changes and evaluate treatment effectiveness.

Critical patients: about every 5 minutes.
Critical Patient
A patient with life-threatening conditions requiring immediate treatment and rapid transport.
Stable Patient
A patient without immediate life threats who allows time for additional assessment.
Transport Priority
Determination of how quickly a patient requires movement to definitive care.
Clinical Decision Making
Using assessment findings to determine priorities, treatment, and transport decisions.

NREMT focuses heavily on this skill.
