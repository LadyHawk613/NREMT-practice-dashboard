NREMT Progressive Case Study #5 (Revised)

Topic: Pharmacology (Anaphylaxis Management - NREMT Scope)

Step 1 – Initial Dispatch

You are dispatched at 11:30 for a 28-year-old female experiencing a severe allergic reaction. Her friend states, "She ate some peanuts and now she can't breathe and her face is swelling up!"

You arrive to find the patient sitting upright, visibly distressed. She is struggling to speak, and you can hear audible stridor.

Initial Assessment

  • Level of Consciousness: Anxious, restless.
  • Airway: Partially obstructed, audible stridor, hoarse voice.
  • Breathing: Respiratory rate 30/min, labored, wheezing heard on auscultation.
  • Circulation: Radial pulses rapid and weak. Skin flushed and covered in hives.

Question 1: Given the patient's presentation, what is the MOST critical initial medication and route of administration?

  • A. Albuterol via nebulizer.
  • B. Epinephrine 0.3 mg intramuscular (IM).
  • C. Administer oxygen via non-rebreather mask.
  • D. Obtain a detailed medical history from the friend.

Step 2 – More Information

After administering Epinephrine 0.3 mg IM into the lateral thigh, you reassess the patient and gather history.

BP: 90/60
Pulse: 110/min
Resp: 26/min
SpO₂: 92% (Room Air)

History

  • Medical History: Known peanut allergy, mild asthma.
  • Medications: Uses an albuterol inhaler as needed.
  • Events: Ingested peanuts 15 minutes prior to EMS arrival.

Question 2: Despite initial epinephrine, the patient's SpO₂ remains low and breathing is still labored. What is the MOST appropriate next pharmacological intervention?

  • A. Administer a second dose of Epinephrine 0.3 mg IM.
  • B. Administer oral glucose.
  • C. Administer albuterol via nebulizer.
  • D. Initiate an intravenous (IV) fluid bolus.

Step 3 – Patient Condition Changes

After a second dose of Epinephrine and high-flow oxygen, the patient's stridor is gone, but she still has wheezing and hives.

New Assessment Findings:

  • Airway: Patent, no stridor.
  • Breathing: Resp rate 20/min, wheezing still present.
  • Circulation: Radial pulses stronger, BP 100/70.
  • SpO₂: 96% on NRB mask.

Question 3: With the airway patent and perfusion improved, what additional medication would be beneficial to address the remaining respiratory symptoms?

  • A. Oral glucose.
  • B. Activated charcoal.
  • C. Assist with the patient's prescribed albuterol inhaler or administer nebulized albuterol.
  • D. Nitroglycerin sublingual.

Step 4 – En Route

En route to the hospital, the patient is more comfortable. She has received two doses of epinephrine and albuterol.

BP: 110/80
Pulse: 90/min
Resp: 16/min
SpO₂: 99% (on NRB)

Patient reports feeling much better. Hives are fading.

Question 4: Considering the potential for a biphasic reaction in anaphylaxis, what is the MOST important aspect of ongoing care during transport?

  • A. Administering pain medication for residual discomfort.
  • B. Preparing for potential recurrence of symptoms and maintaining readiness for further epinephrine administration.
  • C. Encouraging the patient to sleep to conserve energy.
  • D. Discontinuing oxygen as SpO₂ is now 99%.

Step 5 – Hospital Arrival

You arrive at the emergency department. The receiving physician asks for your report.

Question 5: Which information is MOST critical to convey to the receiving physician, specifically regarding pharmacology?

  • A. The patient's friend was very worried.
  • B. The exact brand of peanuts the patient ate.
  • C. The specific medications administered (Epinephrine, Albuterol), their dosages, routes, times of administration, and the patient's response to each.
  • D. The patient's last menstrual period.

Case Study Complete

Excellent work. You have successfully managed a severe pharmacological emergency within the NREMT scope.

Pharmacology Learning Points (NREMT Scope):

  • Epinephrine: The absolute priority in anaphylaxis. It acts as a bronchodilator and vasoconstrictor. Correct dose: 0.3 mg IM for adults.
  • Repeat Doses: If symptoms don't improve or worsen after 5-10 minutes, a second dose of Epinephrine is indicated.
  • Albuterol: Used to address remaining bronchospasm (wheezing) after life-threatening systemic symptoms are managed.
  • Biphasic Reactions: Symptoms can return after initial treatment. Never stop monitoring until the patient is handed off at the hospital.
  • Reporting: Always report the "Five Rights" of medication administration and the patient's clinical response.
