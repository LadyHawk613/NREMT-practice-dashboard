Rule of Nines: Adult Head
9% TBSA

Entire head and neck account for 9% of total body surface area in adults.
Rule of Nines: Each Arm
9% TBSA each arm

Anterior surface = 4.5%
Posterior surface = 4.5%
Rule of Nines: Each Leg
18% TBSA each leg in adults

Anterior surface = 9%
Posterior surface = 9%
Rule of Nines: Anterior Torso
18% TBSA

Chest and abdomen combined represent 18% in adults.
Rule of Nines: Posterior Torso
18% TBSA

Back and buttocks combined represent 18% in adults.
Rule of Nines: Perineum
1% TBSA

Genital area is approximately 1%.
Adult Severe Burn
Typically ≥20% TBSA

Requires aggressive assessment and transport consideration.
Pediatric Burn Concern
Lower TBSA thresholds matter

Children lose heat faster due to larger surface area compared to body mass.
Normal Adult Respiratory Rate
12–20 breaths/min

Changes may indicate respiratory compromise.
Normal Adult Heart Rate
60–100 beats/min

Tachycardia may indicate stress, pain, or shock.
Capillary Refill
Less than 2 seconds

Delayed refill may indicate poor perfusion (interpret with other findings).
Carbon Monoxide Risk
Closed-space fires

Normal pulse oximetry does NOT rule out carbon monoxide poisoning.
Full-Thickness Burn
Destroys epidermis and dermis

May extend into deeper tissues.
Partial-Thickness Burn
Involves epidermis and part of dermis

Often painful and may blister.
Ventilation Rate: Adult BVM
1 breath every 6 seconds

Approximately 10 breaths/min for adults with a pulse.
