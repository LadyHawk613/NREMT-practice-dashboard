Anatomy
The study of body structures and how they are organized.

NREMT: EMTs use anatomy to understand injuries and emergencies.
Physiology
The study of how body systems function.

Helps EMTs understand how illness and injury affect the body.
Anatomical Position
Standard reference position:

Standing upright, facing forward, arms at sides, palms forward.
Superior
Toward the head or upper part of the body.

Example: The chest is superior to the abdomen.
Inferior
Toward the feet or lower part of the body.

Example: The stomach is inferior to the heart.
Anterior
Toward the front of the body.

Also called ventral.
Posterior
Toward the back of the body.

Also called dorsal.
Medial
Toward the midline of the body.

Example: The nose is medial to the eyes.
Lateral
Away from the midline of the body.

Example: The ears are lateral to the eyes.
Proximal
Closer to the point where a structure attaches to the body.

Example: The elbow is proximal to the wrist.
Distal
Farther from the point of attachment.

Example: The fingers are distal to the wrist.
Epidermis
The outermost layer of skin.

Provides protection from the environment.
Dermis
The deeper skin layer containing blood vessels, nerves, sweat glands, and hair follicles.
Subcutaneous Tissue
The fatty layer beneath the dermis.

Provides insulation and cushioning.
Thermal Burn
A burn caused by heat sources such as fire, steam, or hot liquids.
Chemical Burn
A burn caused by exposure to acids, bases, or other corrosive substances.
Electrical Burn
A burn caused by electrical current passing through the body.

May cause hidden internal injuries.
Rule of Nines
A method used to estimate total body surface area (TBSA) burned.

Used to determine burn severity and treatment needs.
Partial-Thickness Burn
Burn involving the epidermis and part of the dermis.

Often painful with blister formation.
Full-Thickness Burn
Burn destroying the epidermis and dermis.

May appear white, brown, or charred and may have reduced sensation.
