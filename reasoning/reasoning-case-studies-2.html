Case Study 1: Altered Mental Status — Diabetic Emergency vs Stroke

Dispatch: You are dispatched to a private residence for a 64-year-old male with altered mental status. His wife states: "He was outside working in the yard, then he came inside confused and started acting strange." Scene is safe.

Initial Assessment: Patient is sitting in a chair. General appearance: Pale, sweaty, confused.

Primary Assessment:

  • Airway: Open
  • Breathing: Rapid and shallow (Respirations: 24/min, SpO₂: 95%)
  • Circulation: Pulse: 112/min, weak radial pulse, skin cool and clammy
  • Mental Status: Opens eyes spontaneously, answers questions incorrectly

Question 1: What should you do first?

A. Perform a detailed neurological assessment
B. Administer oxygen and assess for immediate life threats
C. Obtain a complete SAMPLE history from the wife
D. Determine if the patient has a history of stroke
Correct Answer: B
Rationale: The patient has altered mental status. Immediate assessment and treatment of ABC problems come before detailed history.

Additional Information: The wife states: "He has diabetes. He takes insulin but sometimes forgets to eat." A blood glucose check shows 38 mg/dL. The patient is awake and able to swallow.

Question 2: What is the most appropriate treatment?

A. Administer oral glucose
B. Give aspirin
C. Place the patient supine and monitor
D. Administer insulin
Correct Answer: A
Rationale: A conscious patient with suspected hypoglycemia who can swallow should receive oral glucose.

Progression: After oral glucose, the patient becomes alert, speech improves, and he states he feels better. His wife says: "He doesn't need to go now. He is fine."

Question 3: What is your best response?

A. Transport is recommended because the cause of the hypoglycemia needs evaluation
B. Allow refusal because symptoms resolved
C. Tell the wife hypoglycemia is harmless once corrected
D. Leave if the patient appears normal
Correct Answer: A
Rationale: Correction of blood glucose does not eliminate the possibility of a serious underlying cause.

New Information: During reassessment, the patient becomes confused again and blood glucose drops to 52 mg/dL.

Question 4: What is the most likely explanation?

A. Stroke has developed
B. The patient is having a panic attack
C. The patient may have a longer-acting insulin effect causing recurrent hypoglycemia
D. The glucose meter must be inaccurate
Correct Answer: C

Case Study 2: Trauma — MVC With Chest Injury and Shock

Dispatch: You respond to a two-car motor vehicle collision. A 37-year-old male was the restrained driver, sitting upright outside the vehicle.

Assessment: Patient states: "My chest hurts, but I think I am okay." Findings: Seatbelt marks across chest, increasing difficulty breathing.

Vital Signs & Findings:

  • Respirations: 30/min | Pulse: 118/min | BP: 104/70 | SpO₂: 89%
  • Chest: Unequal rise and fall, decreased breath sounds on the left

Question 5: Your priority concern is:

A. Broken ribs causing pain
B. Anxiety related to the crash
C. A life-threatening respiratory problem
D. Minor chest trauma
Correct Answer: C

Progression: The patient becomes more distressed with increasing respiratory difficulty, distended neck veins, and trachea shifted away from the injured side.

Question 6: Which condition is most likely?

A. Tension pneumothorax
B. Simple pneumothorax
C. Pulmonary embolism
D. Hyperventilation syndrome
Correct Answer: A

Question 7: Your partner wants to complete a full secondary assessment before moving the patient. What should you do?

A. Agree because secondary assessment is always required first
B. Wait until family arrives
C. Complete the SAMPLE history before transport
D. Begin rapid transport because the patient has a critical condition
Correct Answer: D

During Transport: The patient becomes pale, confused, and hypotensive.

Question 8: Which finding best indicates worsening shock?

A. Increased pain level
B. Altered mental status with decreasing blood pressure
C. Patient requesting water
D. Increased anxiety only
Correct Answer: B

Case Study 3: Pediatric Emergency — Anaphylaxis and Airway Threat

Dispatch: You respond to a daycare for a 4-year-old child having trouble breathing. Teacher states: "He was eating a snack when suddenly he started coughing."

Assessment: Child is sitting upright, crying, difficulty speaking. Vital signs: Respirations 36/min, Pulse 142/min, SpO₂ 90%. Findings: Wheezing, facial swelling, hives.

Question 9: What condition should you suspect?

A. Asthma exacerbation
B. Foreign body obstruction only
C. Anxiety reaction
D. Anaphylaxis
Correct Answer: D

Progression: The child becomes quieter. The wheezing decreases, but the child appears tired.

Question 10: Why is this concerning?

A. The allergic reaction has resolved
B. The airway may be worsening despite reduced wheezing
C. The child is becoming calm
D. Oxygen is no longer needed
Correct Answer: B

Additional Information: The child has a prescribed epinephrine auto-injector.

Question 11: What is the most appropriate action?

A. Wait until the child loses consciousness
B. Provide fluids first
C. Assist with epinephrine administration according to protocol
D. Give only oxygen
Correct Answer: C

After Treatment: The child's breathing improves. Parents request: "Can we just take him home?"

Question 12: Your best explanation:

A. Symptoms may return after temporary improvement
B. Epinephrine permanently prevents recurrence
C. Children never need transport after improvement
D. Transport is optional because he looks better
Correct Answer: A

Case Study 4: Multiple Casualty Incident — Triage and Decision Making

Dispatch: You respond to a warehouse collapse where four patients are found:

  • Patient A: Walking around, minor cuts, angry and yelling
  • Patient B: Severe bleeding from leg, alert, respirations 22/min
  • Patient C: Lying still, not breathing initially
  • Patient D: Severe difficulty breathing, cyanotic lips, chest deformity

Question 13: Which patient should receive immediate attention?

A. Patient D because of respiratory distress
B. Patient B because bleeding is visible
C. Patient A because they are closest
D. Patient C because they appear unconscious
Correct Answer: A

Question 14: Patient B has severe bleeding. What is the most appropriate initial intervention?

A. Provide oxygen only
B. Apply direct pressure to control bleeding
C. Complete a full medical history
D. Delay treatment until all patients are assessed
Correct Answer: B

Question 15: Patient C is not breathing. After opening the airway, the patient begins breathing. The next step is:

A. Ignore the patient because they were initially apneic
B. Give food and water
C. Tag and prioritize based on breathing and circulation status
D. Move the patient immediately without reassessment
Correct Answer: C

Question 16: After the incident, your documentation should include:

A. Your opinion about who caused the accident
B. Only treatments performed
C. Only the patient's complaints
D. Objective findings, assessment, treatment, and patient response
Correct Answer: D
