What Is the NREMT Really Testing?

The NREMT is NOT a memorization exam.

It measures your ability to:

  • Recognize life threats
  • Prioritize patient care
  • Make safe clinical decisions
  • Think like an entry-level EMT

Read the Last Sentence First

Strategy:

  1. Read the question first.
  2. Identify what is being asked.
  3. Read the scenario looking for important clues.

Long scenarios often contain distractors.

What Will Kill This Patient First?

Before answering ask:
  • Airway?
  • Breathing?
  • Circulation?
  • Severe bleeding?
  • Altered mental status?

Treat the Patient, Not the Monitor

  • Normal pulse oximetry does not rule out carbon monoxide poisoning.
  • Blood pressure may remain normal during compensated shock.
  • Skin signs and mental status changes may appear first.

Beware of Distractors

Patient has:
  • Diabetes
  • Hypertension
  • Knee pain

But...

The patient is cyanotic and not breathing.

What matters first?

Stable vs. Unstable

Stable

  • Alert
  • Normal breathing
  • Good perfusion

Unstable

  • Airway compromise
  • Respiratory distress
  • Shock
  • Altered mental status

Common NREMT Traps

Treating pain before airway
Diagnosing instead of treating
Detailed assessment before life threats
Ignoring patient changes

Eliminate Wrong Answers

Remove answers that:
  • Ignore ABCs
  • Delay treatment
  • Delay transport
  • Exceed EMT scope
  • Do not address the priority problem

Think About Transport

Ask:

Does this patient need rapid transport?

  • Airway compromise
  • Major burns
  • Stroke
  • Shock
  • Chest pain
  • Major trauma

CAT Exam Tips

  • Questions change difficulty based on performance.
  • Hard questions may mean you are performing well.
  • Do not overanalyze difficulty.
  • Treat every question independently.

Eight Questions Every EMT Should Ask

  1. Is the scene safe?
  2. What is the life threat?
  3. Are the ABCs adequate?
  4. What should I do first?
  5. Does the patient need rapid transport?
  6. What intervention is within my scope?
  7. What changed?
  8. What should I reassess?

Final Challenge

A 68-year-old male with chest pain suddenly becomes unconscious while obtaining SAMPLE history.
The patient condition changed. Priority: 1. Reassess responsiveness 2. Check airway/breathing/circulation 3. Begin appropriate life-saving intervention

Top 10 Test Day Tips

  1. Read carefully
  2. Read the question first
  3. Think ABCs
  4. Find the life threat
  5. Treat the patient
  6. Choose immediate actions
  7. Stay within scope
  8. Reassess
  9. Do not change answers without reason
  10. Stay calm
