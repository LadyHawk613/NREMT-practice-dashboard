Lesson 14: Integrated NREMT Scenarios

Select All That Apply Practice

Question 1 — Respiratory Failure Recognition

You are assessing a 62-year-old patient with difficulty breathing.
  • RR: 34/min
  • Accessory muscle use
  • SpO₂: 86%
  • Unable to speak full sentences | Increasing fatigue

Which findings indicate possible respiratory failure? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Respiratory failure is indicated when the patient can no longer maintain adequate breathing. Tachypnea and accessory muscles indicate distress, while fatigue and inability to speak indicate worsening failure.

Question 2 — Primary Assessment Priorities

You arrive at a motor vehicle collision.

Which actions are appropriate during the initial assessment? Select all that apply.

Rationale: The EMT must first identify hazards, understand the mechanism, and identify threats. SAMPLE and detailed exams occur later.

Question 3 — Shock Recognition

A trauma patient has:
  • HR: 124 | Cool pale skin | Anxiety | Weak radial pulse | BP: 118/82

Which findings suggest developing shock? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Blood pressure may remain normal during compensated shock, while tachycardia, cool/pale skin, weak pulse, and anxiety point toward early shock.

Question 4 — Airway Threats

Evaluate clinical indicators for potential airway compromise.

Which findings suggest a possible airway problem? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Airway compromise may occur before complete obstruction; hoarseness, drooling, and facial swelling point directly to airway threats.

Question 5 — Pediatric Assessment

You are assessing a 3-year-old child.

Which findings require immediate concern? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Children may compensate until they suddenly deteriorate. Stridor and drooling suggest severe airway compromise, and refusing to lie down indicates respiratory distress.

Question 6 — Stroke Assessment

A 75-year-old patient suddenly develops weakness.

Which findings support possible stroke? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Normal glucose helps rule out hypoglycemia as the cause of neurological deficits, while facial droop, slurred speech, and unilateral weakness support a stroke.

Question 7 — Burn Patient Priorities

A patient was rescued from a house fire with burns to chest/arms, soot around mouth, hoarse voice, and singed facial hair.

Which actions are appropriate? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Airway compromise takes priority over burn calculations and wound care. Inhalation injury must be suspected given the presentation.

Question 8 — Geriatric Patient Assessment

An 82-year-old patient fell, takes anticoagulants, is confused, and vomited once.

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Geriatric presentations can mask severe trauma. Anticoagulants heighten intracranial hemorrhage risks, making comprehensive transport and evaluation crucial.

Question 9 — EMT Scope of Practice

Evaluate appropriate actions within the standard EMT scope of practice.

Which actions are appropriate for an EMT? Select all that apply.

Rationale: EMTs perform assessments, provide oxygen, and assist with specific prescribed medications per protocol, but do not diagnose medical conditions or bypass protocols.

Question 10 — Scene Size-Up

At an industrial accident scene:

Which actions are appropriate? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Scene size-up requires hazard checks, resource evaluation, patient counts, and MOI determination while strictly maintaining personal safety.

Question 11 — Respiratory Treatment Decisions

A patient is breathing inadequately.

Which actions may be appropriate? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Inadequate breathing requires immediate intervention (BVM/oxygen) and continuous monitoring rather than delaying care for history or full vitals.

Question 12 — Multiple Casualty Incident

You arrive at an accident scene with multiple patients.

Which patients require the highest priority? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Immediate life threats (airway, breathing, circulation issues like severe bleeding, respiratory distress, and AMS) receive top triage priority.

Question 13 — Reassessment

After providing treatment during transport:

EMTs should: Select all that apply.

Rationale: Reassessment involves repeating vitals, checking intervention efficacy, adjusting treatment, and documenting changes.

Question 14 — NREMT Test Strategy

When unsure of an answer on the NREMT exam:

Students should: Select all that apply.

Rationale: NREMT testing heavily rewards prioritizing ABCs, recognizing immediate threats, and eliminating choices outside standard scope.

Question 15 — Final Clinical Judgment Scenario

You arrive for an unconscious patient. Findings: Airway open, breathing shallow, pulse present.

Which actions are appropriate? Select all that apply.

Rationale: Shallow breathing in an unconscious patient demands assisted ventilations and ongoing monitoring of treatment response before history or secondary exams.
