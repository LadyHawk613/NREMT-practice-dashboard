NREMT Decision-Making Vocabulary

Click each card to reveal the definition and exam importance.
Perfusion
The delivery of oxygenated blood to body tissues.

NREMT clue: Altered mental status, cool skin, weak pulses may indicate poor perfusion.
Shock
A condition where tissues do not receive enough oxygen.

Think: ABC problems, poor perfusion, rapid transport.
Hypoxia
Low oxygen available to tissues.

Signs: cyanosis, altered mental status, respiratory distress.
Dyspnea
Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath.

NREMT priority: determine if airway or breathing is threatened.
Tachypnea
Abnormally rapid breathing.

May indicate respiratory distress, shock, or metabolic problems.
Bradypnea
Abnormally slow breathing.

Concern: inadequate ventilation and respiratory failure.
Altered Mental Status
A change from normal thinking, awareness, or behavior.

Common causes: stroke, hypoxia, glucose problems, trauma.
Compensated Shock
The body maintains blood pressure despite poor perfusion.

Early signs: anxiety, tachycardia, cool skin.
Decompensated Shock
The body can no longer maintain circulation.

Signs: hypotension, confusion, weak pulses.
Occlusion
A blockage of a vessel or airway passage.

Examples: stroke, heart attack, airway obstruction.
Patent Airway
An airway that is open and allows air movement.

Always confirm airway before other treatment decisions.
Ventilation
Movement of air into and out of the lungs.

Problem: inadequate breathing requires intervention.
Respiration
Exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

Remember: breathing does not always equal oxygen exchange.
Crepitus
A crackling sensation caused by air under the skin or bone fragments.

Often associated with trauma.
Guarding
Involuntary tightening of abdominal muscles.

May indicate internal abdominal injury.
Distention
Abnormal enlargement or swelling.

Examples: abdominal distention, jugular vein distention.
Anaphylaxis
Severe allergic reaction affecting airway, breathing, and circulation.

Priority: rapid recognition and epinephrine when indicated.
CVA
Medical term for stroke.

Look for FAST signs and last known well time.
Mechanism of Injury
How an injury occurred.

Helps predict hidden injuries before symptoms appear.
Triage
Sorting patients based on severity and available resources.

Treat the greatest number of patients effectively.
