Airway, Respiration, and Ventilation

Key Concepts

Distinguishing between airway management, respiration, and ventilation is a high-yield concept for the NREMT cognitive exam.

  • Airway: Ensuring the passage from the nose/mouth down to the lungs is patent and clear of obstructions (blood, vomitus, structural collapse).
  • Ventilation: The mechanical process of moving air in and out of the lungs (inspiration and expiration).
  • Respiration: The actual exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide at the cellular and alveolar levels.
NREMT Exam Tip: Remember that a patient can be ventilating (moving chest wall) but not respiring effectively (e.g., carbon monoxide poisoning or severe pulmonary edema blocking gas exchange). Always treat hypoxia promptly with appropriate oxygen therapy.

Vitals & Oxygen Delivery Benchmarks

  • Normal Adult Respiratory Rate: 12–20 breaths/minute.
  • Nasal Cannula Flow Rate: 1–6 Liters per minute (LPM), delivering roughly 24%–44% $O_2$.
  • Non-Rebreather Mask (NRM) Flow Rate: 10–15 LPM, delivering up to 90%+ $O_2$.
  • BVM (Bag-Valve Mask) with Reservoir: 15 LPM, delivering nearly 100% $O_2$.
