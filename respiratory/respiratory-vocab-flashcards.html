Vocabulary Review — Set 1

Click a card to flip it. Try to recall the definition before you flip.

Airway
Passage that allows air movement into and out of the lungs
Patent airway
An open airway free of obstruction
Respiration
Exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide at the alveoli and tissues
Ventilation
Mechanical movement of air into and out of the lungs
Oxygenation
Delivery of oxygen to the blood and tissues
Respiratory distress
Increased effort of breathing that is still adequate
Respiratory failure
Inadequate breathing causing oxygen and/or CO₂ derangement
Hypoxia
Insufficient oxygen available to the tissues
Hypercapnia
Excess carbon dioxide retained in the blood
Apnea
Absence of breathing
Note — Distress versus failure and oxygenation versus ventilation are the pairs most often confused on the exam.

Vocabulary Review — Set 2

Click a card to flip it. Try to recall the definition before you flip.

Dyspnea
Difficulty breathing
Tachypnea
Rapid breathing rate
Bradypnea
Slow breathing rate
Cyanosis
Bluish skin/mucosa from poor oxygenation
Accessory muscles
Neck/chest/abdominal muscles used with labored breathing
Tripod position
Leaning forward on arms to improve breathing
Wheezing
High-pitched sound from narrowed bronchioles
Stridor
Harsh high-pitched sound from upper airway obstruction
BVM
Bag-valve-mask for positive-pressure ventilation
CPAP
Continuous positive airway pressure to keep alveoli open
EXAM NOTE — Stridor points upward to the airway; wheezing points downward to the bronchioles. The location of the sound decides your intervention.
