The Patient With More Than One Problem — High-Level Clinical Decision-Making

Directions & Student Instructions

At each stage, several choices may represent reasonable EMT actions. Select the BEST answer based on the patient's current condition and the priorities of the EMT.

  • Do not choose an answer simply because it is appropriate. Choose the action that is most appropriate at this moment.
  • Do not assume the first explanation is the correct one.
  • Consider the patient's current condition, not just the chief complaint.
  • Pay attention to changes in vital signs and mental status.
  • Once you submit an answer, assume you cannot go back and change it.

This challenge consists of 11 progressive scenarios.

Key Takeaway & Meta-Reasoning:

The EMT should continuously integrate new findings, recognize changes from baseline, reprioritize immediate threats, and modify assessment and treatment as the patient's condition changes.

Core Decision Rule: Is the patient stable? Continue focused secondary assessment. Is the patient changing? Reassess. Is the patient deteriorating? Return to primary assessment.
